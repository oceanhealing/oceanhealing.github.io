I used to think being present meant having a quiet mind.

No distractions. No worries about tomorrow. No conversations replaying in my head. Just being here, fully and effortlessly.

But the more I explore presence, the more I realize it isn't about making the mind silent.

Thoughts come and go. Feelings rise and fall. Sometimes the body is restless, sometimes the heart feels heavy, and sometimes we find ourselves somewhere between remembering and anticipating.

Perhaps presence is not the absence of all this.

Perhaps it is the willingness to notice.

To feel the ground beneath our feet. To become aware of our breathing. To listen to someone without preparing our response. To recognize what is happening inside us without immediately needing it to be different.

Presence is a practice of returning.

We leave the moment countless times, and countless times we can gently find our way back.

There is no perfect way to do it. No achievement to unlock. No finish line waiting for us.

Just this breath, this body, this moment.

And the possibility of meeting it a little more fully.
